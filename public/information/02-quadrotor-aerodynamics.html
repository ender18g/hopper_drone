From four rotors to six degrees of freedom

How an X-quadrotor flies

Build an accurate mental model of thrust, torque, coordinate frames, and the X-configuration using the supplied paper’s NED/FRD convention.

Lesson 02 of 9 28 min Math + physics

Learning objectives

  1. Distinguish the inertial NED frame from the body-fixed FRD frame.
  2. Read the paper’s pose, velocity, and motor-input vectors correctly.
  3. Explain hover, translation, and rotation using force and torque balance.
  4. Switch to an intuitive altitude-up variable without silently changing signs.
01 / 06

The X is geometry, not decoration

In an X configuration, the forward and right body axes pass between the arms. Each rotor is offset in both body x and body y, so each can contribute to roll and pitch torque.

Body axis

b₁ points forward

The front direction moves with the aircraft. When the drone yaws, b₁ rotates in the room.

Body axis

b₂ points right

Right is defined from the aircraft’s own perspective, not the viewer’s.

Body axis

b₃ points down

Forward-Right-Down is right-handed: b₁ × b₂ = b₃.

Axis offset

d is not the arm radius

With dₓ = dᵧ = d, the physical center-to-rotor radius is √2d.

Rotor-arrow caveat

The paper’s yaw row is unambiguous, but its figure does not define whether curved arrows show propeller spin or the resulting body reaction. The lesson follows Equation (10)’s torque signs and does not reinterpret those arrows.

NED and FRD make down positive

The supplied model uses a North-East-Down inertial frame and a Forward-Right-Down body frame. This convention is common in aviation, but it changes signs compared with a classroom z-up graph.

FrameAxesMoves with aircraft?Positive vertical
Inertial frame aa₁ north, a₂ east, a₃ downNoDown
Body frame bb₁ forward, b₂ right, b₃ downYesDown
Normalized Hopper Studio vision coordinatesx right, y up after normalization; raw image pixels use x right, y downCamera-relativeNot an altitude axis

The rotation matrix Rab(ϕ)R_{ab}(\boldsymbol{\phi}) maps a vector written in body coordinates into inertial/NED coordinates. For ZYX Euler angles, the paper defines

Rab(ϕ)=Rz(ϕ3) Ry(ϕ2) Rx(ϕ1)R_{ab}(\boldsymbol{\phi}) = R_z(\phi_3)\,R_y(\phi_2)\,R_x(\phi_1)

Equation (12). This is the standard ZYX (yaw-pitch-roll) convention. In a matrix-vector product, the rightmost roll rotation acts first, followed by pitch and yaw.

Sign trap

At zero yaw, small roll/pitch, and near hover (FT≈mgF_T\approx mg), the paper’s convention gives x¨≈−gϕ2\ddot{x}\approx -g\phi_2 and y¨≈+gϕ1\ddot{y}\approx +g\phi_1. Copying a z-up formula without changing signs produces the wrong acceleration direction.

03 / 06

Twelve numbers describe rigid-body motion

The paper groups configuration into a six-entry pose vector and velocity into a second six-entry vector.

η=[ξaϕ]=[xyzϕ1ϕ2ϕ3] ⁣⊤\boldsymbol{\eta} = \begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\xi}_a\\\boldsymbol{\phi}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}x&y&z&\phi_1&\phi_2&\phi_3\end{bmatrix}^{\!\top}

Equation (5): inertial position plus roll, pitch, and yaw.

ν=[vaωb]=[vxvyvzpqr] ⁣⊤\boldsymbol{\nu} = \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}_a\\\boldsymbol{\omega}_b\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}v_x&v_y&v_z&p&q&r\end{bmatrix}^{\!\top}

Equation (6): inertial linear velocity plus body angular velocity.

The complete first-order dynamic state therefore has twelve scalars, [η⊤  ν⊤]⊤[\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\top}\;\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\top}]^{\top}. The body rates p,q,rp,q,r are not generally identical to roll, pitch, and yaw derivatives.

ξ˙a=va,ϕ˙=J(ϕ) ωb\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_a=\mathbf{v}_a,\qquad \dot{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=J(\boldsymbol{\phi})\,\boldsymbol{\omega}_b

Equations (1) and (2). The Euler-rate Jacobian J maps body angular rate into angle rate.

J(ϕ)=[1sin⁡ϕ1tan⁡ϕ2cos⁡ϕ1tan⁡ϕ20cos⁡ϕ1−sin⁡ϕ10sin⁡ϕ1/cos⁡ϕ2cos⁡ϕ1/cos⁡ϕ2]J(\boldsymbol{\phi}) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \sin\phi_1\tan\phi_2 & \cos\phi_1\tan\phi_2\\ 0 & \cos\phi_1 & -\sin\phi_1\\ 0 & \sin\phi_1/\cos\phi_2 & \cos\phi_1/\cos\phi_2 \end{bmatrix}

Equation (11). This Euler-angle representation is singular when pitch reaches ±90°.

04 / 06

Gravity and rotated thrust set translation

Each rotor accelerates air approximately along body +b₃, so reaction thrust on the aircraft acts along −b₃. At level attitude these directions coincide with inertial down and up, respectively.

Mv˙a=a3mg+Rab(ϕ) TΣuM\dot{\mathbf{v}}_a = \mathbf{a}_3mg + R_{ab}(\boldsymbol{\phi})\,T_{\Sigma}\mathbf{u}

Equation (3): inertial gravity plus body thrust rotated into the inertial frame.

u=[ω1∣ω1∣ω2∣ω2∣ω3∣ω3∣ω4∣ω4∣] ⁣⊤\mathbf{u} = \begin{bmatrix} \omega_1|\omega_1|&\omega_2|\omega_2|& \omega_3|\omega_3|&\omega_4|\omega_4| \end{bmatrix}^{\!\top}

Equation (7). For fixed-direction, nonnegative rotor-speed magnitudes, each entry reduces to ωᵢ².

At level hover, the vertical forces balance:

∑i=14cti ωi2=mg\sum_{i=1}^{4} c_{ti}\,\omega_i^2 = mg

Hover is balance, not zero thrust. If total lift is smaller than weight, the NED z acceleration is positive—downward.

Tilt redirects part of the thrust horizontally. If we introduce an intuitive altitude-up variable h=−zh=-z and a positive total lift magnitude FTF_T, then

mh¨=FTcos⁡ϕ1cos⁡ϕ2−mgm\ddot{h}=F_T\cos\phi_1\cos\phi_2-mg

This is the altitude-up form. It is equivalent to the paper’s NED equation after defining h = −z; it is not the paper’s z equation.

Why tilted hover needs more thrust

The vertical part is FTcos⁡ϕ1cos⁡ϕ2F_T\cos\phi_1\cos\phi_2. As ∣ϕ1∣|\phi_1| or ∣ϕ2∣|\phi_2| grows away from zero within the normal ∣ϕ∣<90∘|\phi|<90^\circ flight range, the cosine product shrinks, so total lift must increase to keep the same altitude.

Unequal rotors create torque

Translation depends on the sum of thrusts. Rotation depends on how those thrusts and drag torques are distributed around the center of mass.

06 / 06

A percent command is not a force

Hopper Studio exposes classroom commands such as 15% forward power. The paper uses rotor channels ωi∣ωi∣\omega_i|\omega_i|, which reduce to ωi2\omega_i^2 for fixed-direction, nonnegative speed magnitudes. A flight controller and motor mixer sit between those descriptions.

  1. Your program requests a direction, duration, or signed axis percentage.
  2. The onboard controller interprets that request as desired attitude/rate behavior.
  3. A mixer distributes the request across four motors.
  4. Motor speed, battery voltage, propeller aerodynamics, mass, and disturbances determine the actual force.
Model responsibly

Do not substitute a Studio power percentage directly for ωi∣ωi∣\omega_i|\omega_i| (or ωi2\omega_i^2 in the nonnegative-speed simplification), newtons, or RPM. It is a normalized control command, and its physical meaning depends on the controller and vehicle.

Check your understanding · If the aircraft pitches nose-down in the paper’s FRD convention, what sign is φ₂?

Nose-down pitch is ϕ2<0\phi_2<0. At zero yaw, small angle, and near hover, x¨≈−gϕ2\ddot{x}\approx-g\phi_2, so the forward x acceleration is positive.
